## What the numerical comparison establishes

Exact identity is not required for a scientific port. Checksums identify inputs
and software; PB/MC agreement is interpreted with convergence and sampling
uncertainty. Identical fixed coordinates isolate architecture/compiler effects
from changing MD structures. PB is generally deterministic for fixed inputs,
but floating-point arithmetic and stopping behavior can differ. MC is stochastic;
identical final microstates are explicitly not an acceptance requirement.

| Same-profile x86 versus ARM comparison | Max intrinsic pK difference | Max interaction difference (kBT) | Max matched-seed occupancy difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Standard: maxc 0.01, coarse linit 100 | 0.013410 | 0.0102202 | 0.001070001 |
| Tighter: maxc 0.0001, coarse linit 1000 | 0.000950 | 0.000301863 | 0.000090003 |

The original engineering screens were frozen **before** either initial run:
absolute intrinsic-pK limit 0.005; interaction tolerance 0.001 kBT plus relative
0.0001; mean-occupancy limit 0.02. They are regression screens, not calibrated
physical accuracy limits. The standard profile misses the strict energy/pK
screens but passes execution, shape/state validity and occupancy screening.
The tighter-profile comparison **passes those same original limits**. No limits
were relaxed to fit the observations. Native tighter-profile job **2150869**
completed `0:0` in **2:00**. Final-image job **2150979** then repeated the
tighter profile **inside the candidate SIF with no solver/runtime override**,
completed `0:0` in **2:57**, and passed the same comparison. Its scientific
output files are byte-identical to the native tighter-profile prototype.

Changing numerical settings on a single architecture had a larger effect than
the original architecture gap: maximum pK shifts **0.125661 (x86)** and
**0.119389 (ARM)**; maximum interaction shifts **0.110201/0.106569 kBT**.
This is empirical sensitivity to the joint maxc/coarse-iteration change, not a
rigorous energy-error bound or attribution to one parameter alone.

The tighter profile is **not demonstrated fully converged**: 162/270 x86 and
161/270 ARM solves reached their 500-iteration focus/model cap with last printed
max change above the requested 0.0001; the largest was 0.00048828125. All logs
and actual parameters are retained. The result demonstrates architecture
agreement for the tested settings, not absolute PB convergence or agreement
with experimental pKas. Further physical convergence work is separate from
establishing a usable native execution path.

## MC sampling uncertainty

Using the saved standard-profile x86/ARM energy tables, the same x86 PETIT
engine sampled three **independent, disjoint seeds per table**: x86-table seeds
1729/2718/3141; ARM-table seeds 91729/92718/93141. Each had 1,000 equilibration
and 100,000 production steps, with PETIT `-t 100` autocorrelation/error estimates.
Combined independent-chain standard errors use `sqrt(sum(SE_i^2))/3`, then
quadrature across tables.

The largest difference between three-chain mean site occupancies was
**0.000863337**. Every observed difference was within **1.4885 estimated combined
standard errors**, consistent with sampling variation. This is a diagnostic,
not a formal equilibrium/equivalence proof: there are only three replicates,
correlations are truncated at lag 100, and saturated sites with zero empirical
error can still have unsampled rare transitions. These uncertainty estimates
are conditional on the saved PB energies; the processor itself was held fixed
for this diagnostic. The actual cross-architecture PETIT executions are retained
separately in the seeded native/reference comparisons.

Amber14SBpH, free termini, four disulfides, pH 6.0, 310 K and 0.1 M PB salt.
